Three disciplines, one schedule

Most plans train lifting and running as rivals. We sequence them so hard days stay hard, easy days stay easy, and recovery is planned.

Lift

Heavy compound lifts progressed by RPE, with volume matched to your training age so you build strength without sacrificing your runs.

Run

Mostly conversational aerobic work, plus one quality session a week. Long runs and intervals are placed away from your heaviest leg days.

Fuel

Calories and macros calculated from your body, lifestyle and goal, with carbohydrates timed around your hardest sessions.

Built on the research

The method follows well-replicated findings, not trends. This is an educational summary, not medical advice.

Manage interference

Concurrent training can blunt some strength and size gains, mainly when running volume is high. Sequencing and spacing sessions reduces the cost.

Wilson et al., 2012
Protein drives adaptation

Intakes near 1.6 g/kg per day capture most resistance-training gains, with higher targets useful when cutting calories.

Morton et al., 2018
Mostly easy, sometimes hard

Elite endurance athletes tend to do most sessions at low intensity and a smaller share hard, which we mirror at every level.

Seiler, 2010
